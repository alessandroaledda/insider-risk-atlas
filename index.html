The Insider Threat Matrix™ is an open framework for computer-enabled insider threat investigations. It describes insider activity in five articles, motive, means, preparation, infringement, and anti-forensics, so that an event can be reconstructed from its first step to its aftermath. Its unit is the entry.

The Insider Risk Map is a layer above it, for the people who design, build, and keep the controls: security architects, detection engineers, the insider threat function. Its unit is the scenario, placed where an organization can act on it. Each scenario is placed on an access population, an act, and the primary asset the act reaches.

Act and asset

Exfiltration: personal data
Data about customers, employees, or other people leaves the organization's control
Exfiltration: business information
Secrets, intellectual property, or inside information leave the organization's control
Sabotage: systems and operations
Systems, processes, or operations are disrupted or destroyed
Sabotage: data integrity
Records, datasets, or models are altered so that decisions rest on false data
Fraud: money and physical assets
Funds, goods, or value are diverted through a process or an authority
Misconduct towards people: personal data
A person's data is accessed, used, or shared without a business reason

Access population

Customer-facing
Customer data, accounts, relationships
Operations
Processes, payments, orders, plants, logistics
Technology
Systems, identities, configurations, source code
Data and analytics
Datasets, models, and reports in bulk
Management
Strategy, prices, decisions, inside information
People functions
Employee data, pay, performance, investigations
Third parties
Contractor and supplier access, guest identities
Non-human identities
Service accounts, agents, and automations under their own identity

An act on an asset

Six combinations of act and primary asset, because six combinations change what is observed and what follows. Data leaving is seen on its movement and answered with the evidence of what left. A system damaged is seen on changes and destructive actions and answered with a restore. Records altered are seen on writes outside the process and answered by restoring the version. Money diverted is seen in the business application, and answered with a payment hold. A person harmed is seen on access to records without a business reason and answered through the Data Protection Officer and the person’s rights.

The motive is a property of the scenario, not of the row: a client list leaves the organization the same way whether it is sold, carried to the next employer, or printed to be worked on at home. The motive changes the consequence; the act and the asset change the controls. A scenario is assigned to one population and to one act on its primary asset, and is counted once; where it reaches a second asset, the card says so and the obligations of both assets apply.

Intent is a property of the scenario, not of the row

Negligent, on this map, means no intent to harm: the act is meant, the exposure is not. Deliberate or negligent, the alert is often the same; the consequence and the lever differ. On the map every scenario is one line, solid where deliberate, dashed where negligent; the counts beside the names are scenarios documented in this version. They measure neither likelihood, nor severity, nor an organization’s coverage.

A population is defined by what its access reaches

A department name would tie the map to one organization; the reach of the access makes one map fit a bank, a utility, and a manufacturer.

The populations overlap, and an organization maps its own roles to them. Where a person belongs to more than one population, the population is the access the scenario uses. Customer-facing access reaches one customer at a time, and a bulk export from it is a signal in itself; data and analytics access reaches the whole base in one query: two risks with different signals. Non-human identities, agents and automations acting under their own identity, are a population because their access is real and their principal is not always visible.

The Matrix carries the techniques

Every scenario points to the Insider Threat Matrix™ entries that describe it, motives, means, preparations, and infringements, down to the channel where the scenario states one, with the number of detections and preventions the Matrix holds for each. The technique reference is the Matrix’s; the map holds the scenario. Where an infringement of the Matrix is absent from the map, the coverage table says why: conduct whose monitoring is restricted by European employment and data protection law, a means rather than an act, or another function’s domain. An entry absent from the map can still be cited by a scenario.

Scope

The map covers acts by people and identities with legitimate access, including a colleague using another colleague’s identity: the act is theirs, the identity is the channel. An external attacker using a stolen identity belongs to security operations.

What an organization adds on its copy

Which scenarios apply and which it has seen; for each, whether the act is visible and whether the asset is recognized; the lever it chose: governance, technical, awareness, or referral to another function. Coverage is measured on the copy.
