ATLAS-001  |  Deliberate  |  also business information

A departing relationship manager copies customer portfolio data for use at another employer without authorization

Signals

Indicators for assessment; no individual signal establishes misconduct.

  • Notice or termination date recorded in the human resources (HR) system
  • Bulk download or export from the customer relationship management (CRM)
  • Email to a personal address, upload to personal cloud, copy to removable media
  • Print volume above the usual
Chain of controls
PreventSensitivity labels on customer data; data loss prevention (DLP) block on personal addresses for the departing population
DetectCorrelate recorded departure dates with customer-system exports and supported outbound-transfer or printing events
ContainThe transfer channel used restricted as soon as the movement is seen, under the incident procedure; evidence preserved and the decision recorded; account revocation on the leaving date; device return
RespondPreserve evidence of the transfer; Legal assesses recovery measures and any proposed contact with the recipients

Definition

Insider Risk Atlas classifies illustrative insider risk scenarios by access population, act, and primary asset. Each scenario identifies signals, proposed control measures, and relevant Insider Threat Matrix™ entries. The signals and controls require validation in the organization’s environment.

The Insider Threat Matrix™ is an open framework for computer-enabled insider threat investigations. It organizes entries into five articles: Motive, Means, Preparation, Infringement, and Anti-Forensics. The Matrix is published in two separately versioned parts, Human and Synthetic; the references on this map use the Human Insider Threat Matrix, version 2.13.0.

Act and primary asset

Exfiltration: personal data
Personal data is transferred or disclosed beyond its authorized handling boundary
Exfiltration: business information
Confidential business information is transferred or disclosed beyond its authorized handling boundary
Disruption: systems and operations
Systems, processes, or operations are impaired, interrupted, or destroyed
Disruption: data integrity
Records, datasets, or models are altered or destroyed, compromising their accuracy, completeness, or reliability
Fraud: money and physical assets
Money or physical assets are diverted through deception or misuse of entrusted access or authority
Misconduct towards people: personal data
Personal data is accessed or used for an unauthorized purpose, including disclosure within that misuse

Access population

Customer-facing
Customer data, accounts, and relationships
Operations
Processes, payments, orders, plants, and logistics
Technology
Systems, identities, configurations, and source code
Data and analytics
Analytical access to datasets, models, and reports, including bulk queries
Management
Strategy, prices, decisions, and inside information
People functions
Employee data, pay, performance, and investigations
Third parties
Contractor access, supplier access, and guest identities
Non-human identities
Service accounts, agents, and automations acting under their own identities

Scenario classification

The map uses six combinations of act and primary asset. Each scenario has one primary assignment and is counted once. Additional affected assets are identified in the card. The primary asset identifies the principal affected resource or data property. Intent is recorded for the scenario’s focal human action; motive is included where supported. Disruption is a grouping of this map and includes unintended impairment.

Primary assignment

The primary assignment follows the scenario’s focal harmful action and principal affected resource. A transfer whose focal harm is disclosure is classified as exfiltration. Access or use of personal data for an unauthorized purpose is classified as misconduct towards people; incidental disclosure remains part of that scenario. Financial diversion takes the fraud assignment when it is the principal outcome. A single scenario is counted once, with other affected assets and supporting actions recorded in its card.

Intent

Intent describes the focal human conduct specified in the scenario. Deliberate means that unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, disruption, or diversion is an intended outcome. Negligent denotes an unintended outcome arising from error or unsafe handling in the illustrative scenario; it is not a finding of legal negligence. For non-human identities, the classification concerns the human instruction, configuration, or oversight stated in the card. Where several people are involved, the card identifies whose conduct the label describes.

Population assignment

The primary population identifies the access exercised in the scenario. Non-human identities takes precedence when the action is executed under an agent or service identity; Third parties applies when supplier or contractor access defines the scenario. Other assignments follow the resource access used, rather than the person’s department. The card identifies other participants where relevant.

Matrix references

Each reference identifies a Matrix entry and the action or condition it describes in the scenario. A channel-specific entry applies only to the stated channel. Counts show the detections and preventions listed in Matrix version 2.13.0; they do not establish the effectiveness or implementation of a control.

Reporting assessment

The card lists, for each affected asset, the instruments to assess under European law. The assessment considers every affected asset. Each legal obligation applies only when its own conditions are met; an asset category alone neither establishes nor excludes a reporting duty.

Scope

The map covers selected scenarios involving current or former insiders, third parties, and non-human identities using access entrusted through an organizational relationship. It includes retained access and the misuse of another identity by an insider. External attackers using stolen identities are outside the map’s scope. The classification is not an exhaustive taxonomy of insider harm.

Organizational copy

An organizational copy records scenario applicability, observed occurrences, control owners, available evidence, implemented measures, and validation results. Coverage is assessed against the organization’s applicable scenarios.
